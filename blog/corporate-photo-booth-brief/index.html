Corporate planning

What should go in a branded photo booth brief?

Give the vendor one page with the event goal, guest count, room schedule, approved artwork and the name of the person signing off. A clear brief helps marketing, the venue and the booth team plan the same experience.

By Mango’s Photo Booth & Entertainment · November 14, 2026

Name the job you want the booth to do

For an employee celebration, the priority might be getting departments into a group picture. For a sponsor reception, it might be giving guests a print they keep with the sponsor name on it. Write down one outcome the team can observe. A count of prints, for example, describes production. One guest may take several, so it cannot tell you how many individuals attended.

Proof the design in someone’s hand

Send the approved logo file, brand colors, required wording and artwork contact. Ask for a proof at the chosen print size. Check the sponsor name, date and faces at that size. A thin tagline that reads perfectly on a laptop may disappear on a strip. Agree on a final artwork deadline and who can approve changes.

Send this with your request for a quote

“We’re hosting [event] in [room and city] for [guest count]. Guests have [time window] between sessions. We want them to leave with [keepsake]. Our artwork approver is [contact]. Please confirm setup access, what’s included in the quote, and which activity counts you can provide afterward.”

If the team wants guests to share pictures, decide where to display the event handle and which photos you have permission to repost. Ask for marketing permission separately from photo delivery. That gives guests a clear choice and gives your team a clearer measure of interest.
