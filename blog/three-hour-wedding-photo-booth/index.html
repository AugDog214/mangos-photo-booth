Wedding timelines

Which three hours should we book for our wedding?

Choose the stretch when guests can actually walk up, bring a friend and stay for a photo. Mark dinner service, speeches and room changes on the timeline before picking your start time.

By Mango’s Photo Booth & Entertainment · November 14, 2026

Work backward from the pictures you want

If the photos you most want include grandparents and young children, ask when they expect to head home. If you want pictures of several friend groups together, look for the stretch when everyone is in the same room. A long guest list tells you less about this choice than the reception schedule.

Try two windows on the same timeline

Imagine cocktails at 5, dinner at 6 and dancing at 7. Booking 5–8 gives early arrivals a chance to visit and includes the first hour of dancing. Booking 7–10 keeps the booth available later but misses people who leave after dinner. These are sample windows you can place beside your own run sheet.

If cocktails and dancing happen in different rooms, mark the booth’s location next to each time. Ask your venue when guests can enter that room. Confirm any downtime or requested split schedule with your vendor before counting those minutes as available.

Plan for the rush after a toast

When everyone stands up at once, one large family may want a group photo followed by smaller combinations. Choose a spot with space to wait. Have your DJ tell guests where the booth is while it is open. If your ideal window still leaves someone out, ask about extra time before finalizing the run sheet.
