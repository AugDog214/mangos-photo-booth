Guest capacity

How many guests can one photo booth serve?

At Mango’s, a guest-count charge applies only to events with more than 50 guests. To plan booth time, also consider how many people want individual photos and when they’ll have a chance to take them.

By Mango’s Photo Booth & Entertainment · November 14, 2026

Does Mango’s charge more for over 50 guests?

Yes. Our three-hour packages start at $550 for Luxury, $650 for Platinum and $1,400 for Big Kahuna. For events with more than 50 guests, the additional charge depends on your guest count. Send us your expected headcount and preferred package so we can quote the total.

For example, if you expect 80 people at your reception, use 80 when requesting pricing. For planning the booth schedule, it also helps to know whether you expect guests to pose in couples, families or individual portraits.

Does a small party need a minimum number of guests?

For a dinner with 25 guests, start with the pictures people would want. A family portrait with three generations, cousins together and a print for each household can make the booth worthwhile even if it sits quiet between groups. If everyone wants just one group picture, ask whether your event photographer can cover it before you budget for a separate booth.

How to estimate turns for 120 guests

A turn is one group’s visit to the camera. Suppose 90 of your 120 wedding guests want a photo and they come in groups averaging three. That’s about 30 turns. If 10 groups come back for another combination, plan for 40 turns. Spread over two open hours, that calls for 20 turns per hour. Squeeze those same 40 turns into a half-hour cocktail reception and the required pace rises to 80 per hour.

These are example schedules. For your own estimate, divide the number of people who want a photo by the typical group size, round up, then add repeat turns. Divide by the time guests can actually use the booth. If the goal is a separate portrait for every attendee, count one turn per person.

What does a vendor’s hourly estimate count?

Mid America Photo Booth reports averaging 45–50 sessions per hour on its setup. Dan’s Camera City suggests planning for about 50 guests per hour and says the mix of solo guests, couples and groups affects that figure. The units matter. Four friends posing together can mean one session, four people photographed and several print copies.

Ask your operator how long it takes from one group stepping in to the next group being ready. Include the pose sequence, extra copies and where guests collect their prints. Use a pace measured with a setup like yours, and leave room for retakes and families getting settled.

When would a second booth help?

If guests can drift over throughout the evening, opening earlier or inviting tables in waves may give everyone time. If all attendees leave the same Naples hotel meeting at once and must return in 30 minutes, an extra hour after the meeting won’t help that break. Ask about a second camera station if posing is the slow step. If people are waiting for magnets while the camera sits free, a separate pickup spot addresses that line.

When you contact Mango’s, send your expected headcount, whether people will pose together or individually, and the length of your busiest break. Tell us if every guest needs a particular photo or favor. Those details let us discuss a setup and schedule that fit your event.
